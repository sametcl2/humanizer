# English quick guide and worked examples

`patterns.md` explains every pattern. This file is the practical companion for English
text: a one-screen cheat sheet of plain replacements, then before/after rewrites by
genre. The "after" versions only use facts present in the "before" or marked as
placeholders the writer must fill; that is the point. If the original has no facts, the
honest rewrite is short.

## Cheat sheet

| Instead of | Usually write |
|---|---|
| serves as / stands as / functions as | is |
| boasts / features / offers | has |
| plays a pivotal/crucial/key role in | (state what it does) |
| is a testament to / underscores the importance of | (delete, or give the fact) |
| nestled in the heart of | in |
| a rich/vibrant tapestry of | (name the actual things) |
| delve into / dive deep into | examine, look at, cover |
| leverage | use |
| foster / cultivate | build, encourage, (or say how) |
| enhance | improve (and say by how much) |
| align with | match, fit, follow |
| a wide/diverse array of | many, (or the number) |
| in order to | to |
| it's important to note that | (delete) |
| Additionally, / Furthermore, / Moreover, | Also, / (nothing) / start a new paragraph |
| In conclusion, / Overall, / In summary, | (end the piece earlier) |
| experts say / observers note | (name them, or cut) |
| associated with / in connection with | (the actual relationship: worked at, founded, taught) |
| not only X but also Y | X and Y |
| It's not just X — it's Y. | It's Y. |
| from A to B (no real scale) | A, B and C |

Replacing words is the last step, not the method. If a sentence still says nothing after
the swap, delete it.

## Example 1: encyclopedia-style place description

Before:
> Nestled in the heart of the Black Sea region, Ordu stands as a vibrant city with a rich
> cultural heritage. Boasting stunning natural beauty, from lush hazelnut orchards to
> breathtaking coastal views, the city plays a pivotal role in Turkey's agricultural
> landscape, underscoring its importance as a regional hub. Despite facing challenges,
> Ordu continues to captivate visitors and residents alike.

After:
> Ordu is a city on Turkey's Black Sea coast and the capital of Ordu Province. The
> province is one of the country's largest hazelnut producers. [Add: population, year,
> source; the specific challenges, if they matter to the article.]

What changed: brochure words, significance padding, the trailing "-ing" clause, the false
range and the challenges formula are gone. The only concrete claim (hazelnuts) is kept
and stated plainly. Missing facts are marked, not invented.

## Example 2: company bio / press-style paragraph

Before:
> Founded in 2019, Lumora is a groundbreaking startup that leverages cutting-edge AI to
> revolutionize the logistics landscape. The company has garnered significant attention,
> having been featured in TechCrunch, Forbes, and other prominent outlets. With a
> commitment to innovation, sustainability, and customer success, Lumora is poised to
> shape the future of supply chain management.

After:
> Lumora, founded in 2019, makes route-planning software for freight carriers. [Add: what
> the product does in one sentence, customers or numbers.] TechCrunch covered its
> [year] funding round. [Only keep a press mention if you can say what it reported.]

What changed: "groundbreaking", "leverages", "landscape", "garnered", the media-coverage
parade, the triplet of values and the future-tense hype. The rewrite asks for the facts a
reader actually wants.

## Example 3: blog post / opinion

Before:
> In today's fast-paced digital world, remote work has become more than just a trend —
> it's a fundamental shift in how we approach our professional lives. Not only does it
> offer flexibility, but it also fosters a healthier work-life balance. However, it's
> important to note that remote work also comes with its own set of challenges.
> Ultimately, finding the right balance is key.

After:
> I've worked from home for three years. The flexibility is real: I can take my kid to
> school and still start at nine. The cost is that nobody sees what I do unless I write
> it down, so I now send a short note every Friday listing what shipped.

What changed: opener cliché, negative parallelism, em-dash punch, "fosters", the didactic
disclaimer and the recap ending are gone, replaced by a first-hand, specific account.
(For a real piece, those details must come from the writer; here they illustrate the
shape.)

## Example 4: email

Before:
> Subject: Follow-Up Regarding Our Recent Discussion
>
> I hope this message finds you well! I wanted to take a moment to follow up on our
> recent conversation and reiterate my enthusiasm for the opportunity. Please don't
> hesitate to reach out if you have any questions. I look forward to hearing from you!

After:
> Hi Dana,
>
> Thanks for the call on Tuesday. You mentioned the team decides by the 15th; if anything
> else would help before then (references, a work sample), I can send it this week.
>
> Best,
> Sam

What changed: title-case subject line, stock pleasantries and exclamation marks out; one
concrete reference to the actual conversation and one concrete offer in.

## Example 5: Markdown-heavy explainer

Before:
> ## 🚀 Key Benefits Of Composting
> - **Reduces Waste:** Composting significantly reduces landfill waste.
> - **Enriches Soil:** It enhances soil health, fostering robust plant growth.
> - **Saves Money:** It offers a cost-effective alternative to fertilizers.

After:
> ## Why compost
> Food scraps and yard waste make up a large share of household trash [add figure and
> source]. Composted, they become a soil amendment that holds water better than most
> garden soil, so you buy less fertilizer and water less often.

What changed: emoji, title case, bold-label bullets and the forced triplet replaced by two
connected sentences of reasoning. Lists are fine when content is truly list-like; three
one-line claims are not.

## Audit example (short)

Input: Example 1 "before".

> **Verdict:** strong surface signs, almost no substance.
> - "Nestled in the heart of... vibrant... rich cultural heritage" - promotional/brochure language.
> - "plays a pivotal role... underscoring its importance" - inflated significance plus trailing participle commentary.
> - "from lush hazelnut orchards to breathtaking coastal views" - false range.
> - "Despite facing challenges, Ordu continues to captivate" - challenges formula with no challenge named.
>
> **Substance:** no population, date or source; the one checkable claim (hazelnut
> production) is buried. These are signals, not proof of AI authorship.
